# Stage 19 — AI Mathematics

**Time budget:** 6–8 weeks part-time / 3 weeks full-time

## Objectives

The math foundation Stages 20-23 and 25 assume without re-teaching: linear
algebra (what a neural network layer actually *is*, mathematically),
probability/statistics (loss functions, evaluation metrics), and enough
calculus to understand backpropagation as gradient descent, not magic.

## No discrete "project" for this stage

Unlike every other stage, ROADMAP.md doesn't list a build project here —
this is pure foundational math, assessed differently. When you actually
reach this stage, the realistic way to check readiness before Stage 21
is:

1. Work a meaningful chunk of problem sets from **at least** MIT 18.06
   (linear algebra) and 3Blue1Brown's calculus series.
2. Be able to explain, in your own words, without notes: what a matrix
   multiplication represents geometrically, what a gradient is and why
   gradient descent uses it, and what a probability distribution's
   variance tells you.
3. Bring worked problem-set solutions (not just "I watched the videos")
   to a review session when you reach this stage — that's the actual
   evidence bar, consistent with the rest of this course.

No `SPEC.md`/acceptance-criteria checklist is generated for this stage
ahead of time — a proper "checkpoint" here should be built once we know
which specific gaps show up as you go through the material, not
speculated now.
